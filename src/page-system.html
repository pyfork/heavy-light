Heavy Light

Thin words next to heavy ones. Square grey panels, one green for action, orange ticks, mint labels and a yellow sticker for good news. Pictures are a single line, traced from real photographs, that draw themselves as you read.

01

Five principles

  1. Weight carries the hierarchy

    One system font, six weights. Every line that matters puts a 200 next to a 900: "Seven cities. One pass". Size changes less than you'd think; weight does the shouting.

  2. Square, flat, quiet

    No rounded corners, no gradients, one soft shadow, kept for the main button. Light grey panels on white hold the content. The page should feel like a well-printed leaflet.

  3. Each colour has one job

    Green acts. Orange confirms (the tick). Mint labels. Yellow celebrates. Red, amber and green status colours never stand alone: an icon and a word always come with them.

  4. One line per picture

    Illustrations are a single continuous stroke over blocks of flat colour, set slightly off register. They are traced from real photographs, so every tower leans the way the real one does.

  5. Motion follows the reader

    Lines draw as you scroll, at your speed, and a companion line walks down the margin with you. Nothing moves on its own except the first drawing. Reduced motion shows everything finished.

    08

    Line art: two ways to draw

    Both start from a photograph traced with OpenCV (edge detection, Zhang–Suen skeletonisation, a shortest-route walk). Use the stroke for one subject; use the horizon for a set of places.

    Option A

    One stroke, flat colour

    One continuous line over a block set 14 / 10px off register. Draws itself as you scroll. For a single building, object or city.

    Option B

    Skyline horizon

    Solid ink skylines on one ground line, city names reversed out in white. Rises from the ground as you scroll. For a row of places.

    The companion line

    Mark page blocks with data-guide="left" or "right". One stroke runs down that side's margin with a slow hand wobble, loops once as it crosses the 140px gap to the other side, and draws up to 72% of the way down the screen, with a green pen dot at its tip. See it on the landing page. On a TOC page the rail's spine plays the same part.

    Use it well

    • One drawing per section, never two competing.
    • Trace real things; credit the photo.
    • Blocks behind, line on top, ink colour only.
    • Give every drawing an empty margin to breathe.

    09

    Data and diagrams

    Charts are drawn like the rest: ink marks, hairline axes, the green kept for the one row that matters. Diagrams follow the diagram-design rules, skinned with these tokens.

    Charts

    • One measure per chart; side-by-side columns instead of two axes.
    • Marks in ink, one highlight in green; labels in text tokens, never the mark colour.
    • Label the extremes and the highlight; the tooltip and the table carry the rest.
    • Every chart has a "Show as a table" twin.

    Diagrams

    • Square elbows at 8px radius, no diagonals.
    • Labels on a paper mask, 6–10px off the line.
    • Mint fill and green edge on one node: the idea.
    • Legend as a strip along the bottom.

    11

    Click to copy

    Anything a reader might paste elsewhere copies in one click and says so: "Copied to the clipboard." There are two ways to say it. The note pops up at the bottom and closes itself after 8 seconds, with a bar counting down; press Stay to keep it, hover to pause it. Next to the button puts the same words, with an orange tick, right beside the button that was pressed.

    Chips

    Field

    The note

    8s, then it closes. Stay keeps it. X closes now. If the browser blocks the clipboard, the text is selected instead and the note says how to copy it by hand.

    Next to the button

    Use it for the one main action on a page, such as copying a letter or a pass code: the reader is already looking at the button. It stays until the next copy. Use the note for chips and small icons, where there is no room beside them. Put an empty <span class="ack" role="status"> straight after the button; the script does the rest.
